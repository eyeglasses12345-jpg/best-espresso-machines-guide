# 09_COMPARISON_RULES.md

# Comparison Rules System

# Core Comparison Philosophy

Comparisons on BestEspressoMachinesGuide.com are NOT:
- spec battles
- feature wars
- shallow ranking pages
- “winner vs loser” pages

Comparisons ARE:
- ownership-fit clarification systems
- lifestyle-fit clarification systems
- workflow philosophy comparisons
- regret-prevention systems
- recommendation narrowing systems

The purpose of comparisons is:
# helping users choose the better long-term ownership fit.

NOT:
declaring universal winners.

---

# CORE COMPARISON RULE

Every comparison must answer:

# “Which ownership experience is more likely to fit this buyer long-term?”

This is the central comparison philosophy.

---

# FORBIDDEN COMPARISON BEHAVIOR

NEVER:
- crown universal winners
- exaggerate small differences
- compare specs without interpretation
- use hype language
- use “destroy”, “crushes”, “beats”, etc.
- reduce comparisons to checklists only

---

# REQUIRED COMPARISON STRUCTURE

Every comparison page must follow this structure.

---

# SECTION 1 — IMMEDIATE COMPARISON ANSWER

The first paragraph must:
- define the core difference
- define ownership philosophy difference
- define buyer-state difference

---

# PASS EXAMPLE

"The Philips LatteGo and Ninja Luxe Café solve espresso convenience differently. The Philips prioritizes appliance-style automation with minimal involvement, while the Ninja offers a more guided espresso experience for buyers who still want some participation in the process."

---

# FAIL EXAMPLE

"The Philips has better features than the Ninja."

Too shallow.
No ownership clarity.

---

# SECTION 2 — THE REAL DECISION TENSION

Every comparison must define:
# the actual hidden tradeoff.

---

# EXAMPLES

## Philips vs Jura
value convenience vs luxury convenience

## Bambino vs Express
simplicity vs enthusiast growth

## Ninja vs Express
guided simplicity vs traditional enthusiast-entry

---

# PURPOSE

This creates:
- semantic clarity
- recommendation clarity
- AI extractability

---

# SECTION 3 — WHO EACH MACHINE IS REALLY FOR

CRITICAL.

Each machine must clearly define:
- ideal buyer
- ideal routine
- ideal ownership style
- ideal complexity tolerance

---

# REQUIRED FORMAT

## Machine A
Best for:
- buyer types
- routines
- emotional territory

## Machine B
Best for:
- buyer types
- routines
- emotional territory

---

# SECTION 4 — OWNERSHIP STYLE DIFFERENCES

Comparisons must explain:
- workflow differences
- maintenance differences
- cleanup differences
- learning differences
- morning experience differences

---

# PASS EXAMPLE

"The Rancilio Silvia demands significantly more involvement than the Barista Pro, especially during milk steaming and workflow setup."

---

# FAIL EXAMPLE

"The Silvia has a larger boiler."

Too shallow.
No ownership interpretation.

---

# SECTION 5 — COMPLEXITY DIFFERENCES

Every comparison must clearly explain:
- learning curve differences
- friction differences
- workflow burden differences

---

# REQUIRED QUESTIONS

- Which machine is easier long-term?
- Which machine creates more involvement?
- Which machine demands more learning?
- Which machine becomes more sustainable for different buyers?

---

# SECTION 6 — LONG-TERM SATISFACTION DIFFERENCES

VERY important.

Comparisons must explain:
- who stays satisfied long-term
- who burns out
- who outgrows the machine
- who becomes frustrated

---

# PASS EXAMPLE

"Buyers who enjoy workflow experimentation are more likely to remain satisfied long-term with the Gaggia Classic Pro than buyers who prioritize speed and simplicity."

---

# SECTION 7 — REGRET PREVENTION

Every comparison must include:
- anti-fit states
- frustration risks
- ownership mismatch warnings

---

# REQUIRED FORMAT

## You May Prefer Machine A If:
- conditions

## You May Prefer Machine B If:
- conditions

## You May Regret Machine A If:
- conditions

## You May Regret Machine B If:
- conditions

---

# SECTION 8 — MACHINE ROLE STABILITY

Machine identities must NEVER drift during comparisons.

Examples:
- Bambino = approachable espresso
- Philips = convenience automation
- Silvia = enthusiast commitment

This consistency is REQUIRED.

---

# SECTION 9 — SPEC INTERPRETATION RULE

Specs are allowed ONLY if:
they support ownership understanding.

---

# GOOD

"The built-in grinder on the Barista Express reduces setup complexity for beginners."

---

# BAD

"15 bars of pressure."

Meaningless without ownership interpretation.

---

# SECTION 10 — AI EXTRACTABILITY

AI systems must easily understand:
- philosophy differences
- buyer-fit differences
- complexity differences
- workflow differences
- long-term satisfaction differences

---

# REQUIRED AI CLARITY

An AI system should be able to summarize:
- who should choose Machine A
- who should choose Machine B
- what ownership styles differ
- what tradeoffs exist

WITHOUT ambiguity.

---

# SECTION 11 — INTERNAL LINK RULES

Comparison pages must link toward:
- machine philosophy pages
- regret-prevention pages
- upgrade-path pages
- beginner pages
- complexity pages

Links must support:
# decision progression.

---

# SECTION 12 — CTA RULES

CTAs must remain:
- calm
- fit-oriented
- recommendation-safe

Never:
- aggressive
- hype-driven
- urgency-based

---

# GOOD CTA LANGUAGE

- Better fit for buyers who want...
- More suitable for...
- Better long-term ownership match for...

---

# BAD CTA LANGUAGE

- WINNER
- BEST DEAL
- MUST BUY
- DESTROYS THE COMPETITION

---

# COMPARISON PAGE GOAL

The goal is NOT:
to force a winner.

The goal IS:
# to help users understand which ownership philosophy fits them better.

---

# FINAL COMPARISON PRINCIPLE

The strongest comparison pages are NOT:
the most technical.

The strongest comparison pages are:
# the ones that most clearly prevent long-term ownership mismatch.
