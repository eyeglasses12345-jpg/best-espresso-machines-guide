<h2>What Buyers Usually Get Wrong</h2>

<p>The biggest mistake is assuming expensive machines require less maintenance. In reality, more advanced espresso systems often require more workflow attention.</p>

<p>Another mistake is ignoring grinder maintenance. Grinder retention, stale coffee oils, and inconsistent particle buildup directly affect espresso flavor and extraction consistency.</p>

<p>Many buyers also underestimate milk cleanup. Steam wands, milk systems, and frothing components require regular cleaning or performance declines quickly.</p>

<p>That is why two buyers can own the same machine and have completely different long-term experiences. One buyer builds maintenance naturally into the workflow. Another buyer avoids cleanup until the machine becomes frustrating.</p>

<h2>Basic Espresso Maintenance Habits</h2>

<ul>
<li>Flush the machine after use</li>
<li>Clean the steam wand immediately after milk drinks</li>
<li>Empty the drip tray regularly</li>
<li>Brush excess grounds from the grinder</li>
<li>Descale based on water hardness and usage</li>
<li>Use fresh filtered water when possible</li>
<li>Keep baskets and portafilters clean</li>
<li>Avoid leaving old coffee grounds sitting in grinders</li>
</ul>
