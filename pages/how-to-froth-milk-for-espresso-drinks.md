<h2>Basic Milk Frothing Process</h2>

<h3>Step 1: Start with Cold Milk</h3>

<p>Cold milk gives buyers more steaming control and more time to texture properly before overheating.</p>

<h3>Step 2: Position the Steam Wand Correctly</h3>

<p>The steam wand tip should sit slightly below the milk surface near the side of the pitcher to help create a rolling vortex.</p>

<h3>Step 3: Introduce Air Carefully</h3>

<p>Early in the process, a small amount of air is added to create foam. Too much air creates large bubbles instead of smooth microfoam.</p>

<h3>Step 4: Roll the Milk</h3>

<p>Once air is introduced, the milk should spin in a smooth vortex that blends foam evenly into the milk texture.</p>

<h3>Step 5: Stop Before Overheating</h3>

<p>Overheated milk loses sweetness and texture quality. Smooth milk texture matters more than extreme heat.</p>

<h2>What Buyers Usually Get Wrong</h2>

<p>The biggest mistake is creating large dry bubbles instead of smooth microfoam. This usually happens because too much air is introduced too aggressively.</p>

<p>Another mistake is overheating the milk. Burnt milk loses sweetness and creates poor drink texture.</p>

<p>Many buyers also underestimate steam power differences between machines. Weaker steam systems often require slower technique adjustments.</p>

<p>Another common issue is poor milk-cleaning habits. Steam wands require immediate cleaning after use to prevent residue buildup and clogging.</p>
