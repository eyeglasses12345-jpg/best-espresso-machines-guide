<h2>Common Espresso Problems and Causes</h2>

<h3>Espresso Tastes Sour</h3>

<ul>
<li>Grind too coarse</li>
<li>Under-extraction</li>
<li>Water temperature instability</li>
<li>Shot pulled too quickly</li>
<li>Weak puck prep</li>
</ul>

<h3>Espresso Tastes Bitter</h3>

<ul>
<li>Grind too fine</li>
<li>Over-extraction</li>
<li>Shot pulled too slowly</li>
<li>Burnt or stale beans</li>
<li>Dirty machine or grinder oils</li>
</ul>

<h3>Weak or Watery Espresso</h3>

<ul>
<li>Insufficient coffee dose</li>
<li>Coarse grind size</li>
<li>Channeling</li>
<li>Old beans</li>
<li>Inconsistent tamping</li>
</ul>

<h3>Milk Will Not Froth Properly</h3>

<ul>
<li>Steam wand blockage</li>
<li>Incorrect milk temperature</li>
<li>Poor steam pressure</li>
<li>Incorrect wand positioning</li>
<li>Low-fat milk limitations</li>
</ul>

<h3>Machine Leaks or Drips</h3>

<ul>
<li>Worn seals</li>
<li>Dirty group head</li>
<li>Overfilled drip tray</li>
<li>Loose water tank connection</li>
<li>Poor portafilter seating</li>
</ul>

<h2>What Buyers Usually Get Wrong</h2>

<p>The most common mistake is blaming the machine first instead of troubleshooting the workflow.</p>

<p>In many cases, espresso inconsistency comes from:</p>

<ul>
<li>cheap grinders</li>
<li>stale coffee</li>
<li>poor puck prep</li>
<li>changing grind sizes constantly</li>
<li>lack of machine cleaning</li>
<li>inconsistent dosing</li>
</ul>

<p>Another mistake is expecting instant café-quality espresso without learning extraction behavior. Espresso is sensitive. Small changes can dramatically affect results.</p>

<p>That is why two buyers can own the exact same machine and experience completely different espresso quality.</p>

</ul>
